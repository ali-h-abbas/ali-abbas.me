The daimon in Greek thought is a spirit intermediate between gods and mortals - neither divine nor human but the connective tissue between them. In Plato's _Symposium_, [[Eros]] himself is named a great daimon, ferrying prayers upward and blessings downward; in the [[Myth of Er]], each [[soul]] is assigned a daimon before birth as guardian of its chosen life-pattern. Socrates famously consulted his _daimonion_ - an inner voice that never told him what to do, only warned him what not to - making the daimon the ancestor of both conscience and calling. The Romans translated it as _genius_, the tutelary spirit of a person; only later, through Christian polemic, did "demon" acquire its wholly negative charge.

In [[Neoplatonism]] the daimon becomes part of the architecture of mediation between [[The One]] and the material world. Plotinus taught that the daimon is the level of soul just _above_ the one we consciously live from - so that living well means living up toward one's daimon, and [[theurgy]] and philosophy alike aim at that alignment. The daimon thus personifies the pull of [[Emanation & Descent]] in reverse: the thread by which the descended soul remembers its origin, kin to [[anamnesis]] as recollection and to the [[Nous]] as the intelligence the soul participates in.

Psychologically, the daimon names the experience of being _accompanied_ - of a life having an insistence that is one's own and yet not chosen by the ego. [[Carl Jung]] spoke of the daimonic force of the [[Self]] pressing toward [[individuation]]; James Hillman's [[acorn theory]] makes the daimon its centrepiece, the carrier of the innate image of character and calling that shows up as obsession, symptom, and the persistent return of certain themes in one's private thoughts. Encounters with it border on the [[numinous]]: the daimon does not negotiate, it summons.

Understanding the daimon means holding the intermediate position it was always meant to occupy - between literal spirit-being and mere metaphor, between fate and freedom. It names the felt reality of [[purpose]] that arrives as a call rather than a decision, and the ancient intuition that a life ignored by its daimon grows sick, while a life lived in its service becomes a destiny.
