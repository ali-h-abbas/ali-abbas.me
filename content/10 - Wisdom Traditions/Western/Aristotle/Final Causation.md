Final causation is Aristotle's cause of _ends_: the telos toward which a thing develops, that _for the sake of which_ it is and acts. Why does the acorn grow into an oak? Because that is its end - a future state directing present development. Where [[Efficient Causation]] pushes from the past, final causation _draws from ahead_: the oak is somehow at work in the acorn, the mature form in the embryo, the destination in the desire. For Aristotle this was not mysticism but biology's most obvious datum - living things manifestly _aim_ - and teleology remained explanation's crown until the scientific revolution outlawed it as unverifiable.

The banishment of final causes is modernity's deepest cut, and the one [[the meaning crisis]] traces most of its wounds to. In a cosmos of only [[Material Causation|material]] and efficient causes, [[purpose]] must be an illusion, striving a metaphor, and [[meaning]] something minds paint onto mindless process. Yet the exile was never complete: biology cannot describe a heart without "for pumping blood," evolution smuggles in "function," attractor states in [[systems theory]] and [[feedback loops]] are teleology in mathematical dress, and [[Alfred North Whitehead]]'s [[Process Philosophy]] restores the "lure" of what could be as a genuine causal factor in every occasion of [[becoming]]. The [[Neoplatonism|Platonic]] tradition had its own version all along: [[the Good]] as the final cause of all things, drawing rather than pushing.

Final causation is also the causality of the psyche. James Hillman's [[acorn theory]] is explicitly a teleological psychology - the [[daimon]] as the carrier of a life's end, calling rather than compelling - and the felt pull of vocation, the way certain futures summon us, is final causation experienced first-person. This is the register in which [[Wisdom]] operates: knowing not just how things work but what they are _for_, and aligning [[action]] with ends worth serving.

Understanding final causation means recognizing that "what is this for?" is a question about the world and not only about our preferences - and that systems, lives, and technologies designed as if the question had no answer tend to optimize brilliantly toward nothing.
