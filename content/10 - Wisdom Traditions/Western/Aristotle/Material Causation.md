Material causation is the first of Aristotle's four causes: _that out of which_ a thing is made and from which it derives its possibilities. Why is the sculpture the way it is? Because it is made of marble - and marble can hold an edge that sand cannot, weighs what bronze does not, weathers as wood will not. The material cause is not inert filler; it is the reservoir of potential (_dynamis_) that the other causes work upon. Matter for Aristotle is always matter-_for_-something, intelligible only in relation to the [[form]] it can receive - a view with echoes in alchemy's [[prima materia]], the undifferentiated ground awaiting transformation.

Aristotle developed the notion against his predecessors who made matter do all the explanatory work - the pre-Socratics who reduced everything to water, air, or atoms. His point was that naming the ingredients never explains the recipe: knowing a vase is clay tells you nothing about why it is a _vase_. Material causation is real but radically incomplete without [[Formal Causation]] (the pattern that makes it this rather than that), [[Efficient Causation]] (the agent that brought it about), and [[Final Causation]] (the end it serves).

Modern science retained material causation - promoted it, even, into one half of its entire causal vocabulary alongside the efficient cause. The physicalist [[ontology]] this produces treats reality as nothing _but_ matter in motion, which is precisely the pre-Socratic move Aristotle diagnosed as insufficient: [[reductionism]] explains wholes by ingredients and loses the pattern in the process. The critique running through [[the meaning crisis]] discourse is not that material explanation is false but that it was never designed to be the whole story - [[emergence]] in [[systems theory]] rediscovers, in modern dress, that what things are made of underdetermines what they are.

Understanding material causation means holding both its necessity and its limits: no form without something formed, no purpose without a substrate to carry it - and yet the marble, alone, never explains the David.
