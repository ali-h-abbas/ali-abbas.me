Wisdom traditions are the lineages across cultures that developed systematic ways of cultivating [[Wisdom]] rather than merely transmitting knowledge - ecologies of practices aimed at transformation of the knower, not just information for the known. What unites Stoicism, [[Taoism]], [[Sufism]], [[Neoplatonism]], the Buddhist path of [[Prajñā Wisdom]], [[vedic philosophy]], [[Hermeticism]], [[gnosticism]], and the [[Shamanism|shamanic]] practices that precede them all is a shared wager: that human beings are prone to self-deception and foolishness, that flourishing requires seeing through illusion, and that this seeing can be _trained_. Each tradition pairs a diagnosis of the human predicament - [[dukkha]], sin, forgetfulness, sleep - with a discipline for waking from it.

Their methods converge more than their metaphysics. Across lineages one finds the same functional toolkit: [[Ritual]] and [[initiation]] to mark and provoke transformation, [[meditation]] and [[mindfulness]] to train attention, ascetic and somatic disciplines to work through the body, [[Mythology]] and [[symbols]] to encode what discursive language cannot hold, and community and lineage to distribute the work across generations - wisdom cultivation as [[Distributed Cognition]]. This convergence is what the [[Perennial Philosophy]] reads as evidence of a common summit behind different slopes, though the traditions themselves often insist their differences matter as much as their agreements.

[[John Vervaeke]] situates the renewed interest in wisdom traditions - the [[mindfulness]] revolution, revived Stoicism, psychedelic and contemplative research - as one face of the response to [[the meaning crisis]]. Modernity dismantled the cosmologies these traditions were embedded in while discarding the practices along with them; the current hunger is less for their doctrines than for their lost technologies of transformation. The risk Vervaeke names is bricolage without depth - practices stripped from the ecologies that made them wise.

This folder gathers the traditions as living systems rather than museum pieces: Eastern paths of liberation, Western [[mystery schools]] and [[Western esotericism]], [[contemplative traditions]] East and West, and the bridges and symbols they share. Understanding them together means holding both the perennialist intuition of a common [[Truth]] and the particularist insight that each tradition is a complete ecology whose parts lose potency in isolation.
