Reason is the faculty of inference and coherence - the capacity to move from premises to conclusions, demand consistency, weigh evidence, and give an account (_logos_) of why. In the classical picture it was never mere calculation: [[Plato]] distinguished discursive reasoning ([[Dianoia]]) from direct intellection ([[noesis]]), placing both on a ladder that runs from opinion up through mathematics to [[contemplation]] of the [[form]]s. Reason in this older sense is the [[soul]]'s kinship with the [[intelligible realm]] - the [[Logos]] in us answering to the Logos in the world, which is why the Greeks could treat rationality as _participation_ in cosmic order rather than a private processing skill.

Modernity kept reason but narrowed it. The scientific revolution's restriction of explanation to [[Efficient Causation|efficient]] and [[Material Causation|material]] causes redefined rationality as instrumental: calculating means toward given ends, with the ends themselves declared beyond rational assessment. This is reason as engine rather than eye - powerful for prediction and control, silent on [[meaning]], [[purpose]], and value. The [[the meaning crisis]] critique holds that this instrumental narrowing is itself unreasonable: a faculty that once adjudicated what is _worth wanting_ now merely optimizes whatever is wanted, and [[Wisdom]] - the discrimination of ends - falls outside its remit.

Contemporary [[Cognitive Science]] complicates the picture from another side. Reasoning runs on machinery it did not choose: [[perception]], [[attention]], [[Emotion]], and [[embodied cognition]] shape what shows up as evidence at all, and the same adaptive processes that make us intelligent generate systematic self-deception - Vervaeke's foolishness, which no amount of raw inference dispels. Reason is also social: argument evolved for persuasion and coordination, functioning best as [[Distributed Cognition]] - [[dialectic]] between minds that catch each other's errors - rather than as solitary computation.

Understanding reason means neither crowning nor dethroning it: it is one mode of knowing that requires the others - perceptual, participatory, embodied, contemplative - to furnish its materials and aim its use. Reason severed from those roots turns rationalization; reason integrated with them is a component of wisdom.
