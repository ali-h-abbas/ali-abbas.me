Agency refers to the capacity to convert intention into effective action, where [[Consciousness]] meets [[causality]] and potential becomes enacted [[change]]. It is not merely the presence of choice, but the ability to align discernment, motivation, and behaviour so action expresses a coherent centre rather than external momentum.

This makes agency distinct from reactivity. When behaviour is driven mainly by social scripting, fear patterns, or unexamined habit, action can still occur without authorship; agency begins when response is selected rather than defaulted. In this sense, agency is closely coupled with [[authenticity]], [[will]], and the regulatory ability to sustain direction across friction, uncertainty, and delay.

Because agency is developmental, it is often restored through contact with constraint: confronting internalized limits, engaging [[shadow]] material, and reclaiming participation in decisions previously outsourced. The key implication is that agency is less a static trait than a trained relation between inner clarity and outer consequence.
