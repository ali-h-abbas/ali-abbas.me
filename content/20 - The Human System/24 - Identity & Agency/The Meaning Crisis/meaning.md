Meaning denotes the quality of significance, coherence, and mattering that connects experience to larger patterns, purposes, or contexts beyond immediate sensation. The term derives from the Old English _mænan_ ("to intend, signify"), pointing toward meaning's dual nature as both discovered pattern and projected intention - a tension that becomes acute in modernity's attempt to separate "objective" fact from "subjective" value. Meaning operates at multiple scales: semantic meaning in language (how signs refer), psychological meaning (what matters to a person), and existential or cosmic meaning (whether reality itself possesses purpose or intelligibility beyond human projection).

The question of whether meaning is intrinsic to reality or imposed by consciousness defines a central philosophical divide. Mechanistic worldviews following Galileo and Newton positioned meaning as strictly human attribution projected onto an indifferent, meaningless cosmos - what Max Weber called the "disenchantment" of nature. Yet this view faces both philosophical difficulties (the performative contradiction of meaningful beings emerging from pure meaninglessness) and existential costs, contributing to [[the meaning crisis]] diagnosed by [[John Vervaeke]]. Alternative frameworks suggest meaning emerges through relationship rather than existing as either purely objective property or pure subjective invention: [[Alfred North Whitehead]]'s [[process philosophy]] proposes that value and aim characterize even basic occasions of experience, while [[Richard Tarnas]] argues for an [[Archetype|archetypal]] cosmology where psyche and cosmos participate in shared patterns of significance.

The concept intersects with [[participatory]] knowing, which dissolves the dichotomy between discovered and created meaning, [[consciousness]] studies exploring how minds generate coherence, and wisdom traditions cultivating what Vervaeke calls "connectedness to reality." It relates to [[purpose]], [[value]], [[identity]], symbolic cognition, and debates between nihilism and various forms of enchantment or reenchantment. Meaning may be less a property possessed than an achievement of attunement - emerging when beings engage reality's depths with appropriate responsiveness.
